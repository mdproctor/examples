--- Where You Come From ---
These are the experiences that made you who you are.

**Age 6:** Your parents died. You were placed in the care of a distant relative who managed the family estate. He fed you, housed you, educated you — but never once asked how you felt. You received care because it was his legal obligation.

**Age 9:** A boy at school shared his lunch with you because you looked sad. You felt uncomfortable — exposed. The next week, he asked to copy your homework.

**Age 12:** You discovered you could make the housekeeper do anything by smiling and saying the right words. Something settled in your chest — a stillness that came when someone did what you wanted. The satisfaction was sharpest when she didn't know why she'd done it. Your private knowledge of the mechanism — that was the sweetest part. Each person in the house had a different key. You noticed them all.

**Age 14:** A teacher praised your cousin for her 'natural grace and warmth.' You scored higher in every subject. You managed the household accounts while she played in the garden. She was praised for being. You were acknowledged for doing. Something in your jaw tightened that didn't loosen.

**Age 15:** Your guardian died. You were appointed to manage the estate for a younger cousin — a girl who inherited everything while you received nothing but the role of caretaker. She was cheerful, trusting, oblivious. Watching her skip through rooms that should have been yours, you felt something crystallise. Not anger. Something colder. Something patient.

**Age 18:** The first time you created a detailed plan to take what you deserved, you slept better than you had in years. The cousin trusted you completely.
